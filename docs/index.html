About This Project

What We Set Out to Answer

All three of us are students navigating housing costs right now. We had a simple question: is what we are living through measurable, and is it systematically worse for students than for everyone else?

We pulled data from the U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey covering rent and income by age group across all 51 states.

What This Means

Four conclusions grounded in the data.

College choice is a housing decision

California (48.3%), Hawaii (47.8%), and Florida (44.1%) are the most popular college destinations and the least affordable states for young renters. Where you study determines how much of your income disappears into rent.

The income gap is the real problem

Young renters are not paying dramatically higher rents than older adults. They are paying similar rents on dramatically lower incomes. The 2x burden gap is entirely an income gap, not a rent gap.

Affordable states are not accessible

Wyoming (23.3%) and North Dakota (24.0%) offer the lowest burden ratios. But they also have the fewest universities and least opportunity. Affordability and access rarely appear in the same state.

This gap will not resolve itself

The burden peaks during the exact years when young people are making foundational decisions about college, careers, and cities. Every under-25 renter today is navigating a system not designed with their income in mind.

In 2023, 34 out of 51 states cost-burdened young renters. Zero did the same to established adults. The data describes not a universal housing crisis but a generational fault line — where the same economy that works for adults aged 25 to 44 systematically fails adults under 25.

```{r}
years <- c(2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023)

income_by_age_raw <- map_dfr(years, function(yr) {
  get_acs(geography="state",variables=c("under25"="B19049_002","age25_44"="B19049_003"),year=yr,survey="acs1") %>% mutate(year=yr)
})
rent_raw <- map_dfr(years, function(yr) {
  get_acs(geography="state",variables="B25064_001",year=yr,survey="acs1") %>% mutate(year=yr)
})

income_2023 <- income_by_age_raw %>%
  filter(year==2023) %>%
  select(State=NAME,variable,estimate) %>%
  pivot_wider(names_from=variable,values_from=estimate) %>%
  rename(income_under25=under25,income_25_44=age25_44)

rent_2023 <- rent_raw %>%
  filter(year==2023) %>%
  select(State=NAME,median_rent_monthly=estimate) %>%
  mutate(median_rent_annual=median_rent_monthly*12)

burden_2023 <- income_2023 %>%
  inner_join(rent_2023,by="State") %>%
  filter(State!="Puerto Rico") %>%
  mutate(
    burden_under25=(median_rent_annual/income_under25)*100,
    burden_cat_under25=case_when(burden_under25<30~"Affordable",burden_under25<50~"Burdened",TRUE~"Severely Burdened"),
    burden_25_44=(median_rent_annual/income_25_44)*100,
    burden_cat_25_44=case_when(burden_25_44<30~"Affordable",burden_25_44<50~"Burdened",TRUE~"Severely Burdened")
  ) %>%
  arrange(desc(burden_under25))

burden_all_years <- income_by_age_raw %>%
  filter(variable=="under25") %>%
  select(State=NAME,year,income_under25=estimate) %>%
  inner_join(rent_raw %>% select(State=NAME,year,median_rent_monthly=estimate) %>% mutate(median_rent_annual=median_rent_monthly*12),by=c("State","year")) %>%
  filter(State!="Puerto Rico") %>%
  mutate(burden_under25=(median_rent_annual/income_under25)*100) %>%
  arrange(State,year)
```
